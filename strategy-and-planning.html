The Approach

The work behind a plan that actually holds.

Grounded in

  • Complex adaptive systems thinking
  • Resilience science
  • Cognitive science

Strategy and planning, done well, is not a document-production exercise.

It’s the disciplined work of building a shared, evidence-based view of the conditions your organisation is actually operating in, then translating that view into the priorities, capability investments, and catalytic actions that will keep you relevant as those conditions continue to shift.

At Resilient Futures, we approach strategy and planning through Strategy in Action (SiA), a living decision-making system grounded in complex adaptive systems thinking, resilience science, and cognitive science.

The intent is not a polished plan that sits on a server. It’s a plan your Board and Executive understand deeply, can defend confidently, and can adapt continuously as conditions evolve.

Where We Engage

Where most leaders engage us.

Strategy and planning engagements take different shapes depending on where an organisation is in its cycle and the conditions it’s facing. We typically work across four areas.

01

New strategic plan development

End-to-end

A co-designed, end-to-end strategy engagement, from conditions analysis through to Board-endorsed priorities, capability investment pathway, and implementation framework.

Best when: the current cycle is ending, or the operating reality has shifted enough that a reset, not a refresh, is required.

02

Refresh or sense-check of an existing plan

Stress-test

A structured stress-test of a current strategic plan against the actual change landscape. Identifies where the plan still holds, where conditions have moved past it, and where adaptive course-correction is needed.

Best when: the strategic direction still has merit, but the assumptions underneath it need testing, without throwing out work that remains sound.

03

Operational and annual planning

Strategy → execution

Translating strategic direction into annual business plans and operational priorities, with the kind of integration between strategy and execution that prevents the annual plan from quietly drifting away from the strategic intent it’s meant to serve.

Best when: a strategy exists at the top, but the annual planning cycle isn’t reliably translating it into what teams actually do.

04

Sub-plan and service planning

Domain-specific

Domain-specific planning at the service, portfolio, or function level, including Council plans, service plans, and sub-plans across growth, innovation, customer, workforce, and technology.

Always: aligned to the broader strategic frame, never built in isolation.
