The Philosophy

Built from a different premise about what strategy is for.

Most strategy processes begin with the assumption that the environment is knowable enough to plan around. Set direction. Build the plan. Execute. Review annually.

Strategy in Action begins from a different premise: that the operating environment is continuously shifting, that change is structural rather than episodic, and that what organisations need is not a better plan — but a better system for making consequential decisions continuously.

SiA does not replace strategic planning. It fundamentally changes its nature — from an event to a capability.

“The goal is not an organisation that has a strategy. It is an organisation that is strategic — continuously.”

Larry Quick · Co-founder, Resilient Futures

SiA was developed over 25 years working with Boards and Executives across Government, Health, Education, and peak bodies — organisations where the complexity of stakeholder maps, regulatory environments, and operational interdependencies makes conventional strategy not just insufficient, but actively misleading.

The Foundations

Grounded in four disciplines conventional strategy ignores.

SiA has been developed and refined over 25 years of working with Boards, Executives, senior leaders and people across Corporations, Government, Not-for-Profit, Associations and Peak Bodies. The organisations we work with sit across a broad range of sectors yet all share the challenge and opportunity of complex stakeholder networks, changing regulatory frameworks, operational interdependencies and real-world pressures that make conventional strategy not just insufficient, but actively misleading.

Discipline 01

Complex Adaptive Systems

The science of emergence and self-organisation.

Systems where many independent agents interact, adapt, and produce outcomes that cannot be predicted by analysing the parts in isolation. Behaviour emerges from interactions, not from any single component.

Organisations and their environments are complex adaptive systems. Strategy must work with emergence — not pretend it can be designed out.

Discipline 02

Cognitive Psychology

How humans actually decide under uncertainty.

The study of how humans perceive, decide, and reason — including the biases, blind spots, and pattern-matching shortcuts that distort judgement when conditions are ambiguous and stakes are high.

Strategic decisions are made by humans under pressure. Methods that ignore cognitive limits produce confident but wrong choices.

Discipline 03

Systems Thinking

Feedback, leverage, and unintended consequences.

A discipline for understanding how parts of a system interact — feedback loops, time delays, leverage points, and the unintended consequences of well-intentioned interventions.

Strategy lives in interdependencies. Linear cause-and-effect models break down precisely when the stakes are highest.

Discipline 04

Resilience Science

Absorbing disturbance without losing function.

The study of how systems absorb disturbance, adapt, and reorganise without losing their essential function — across ecology, engineering, and human organisations.

The goal is not immunity to disruption. It is the capacity to keep generating value through it.

These are not abstract reference points. They are the operating principles built into every standard, decision rhythm, and tool that follows.

The Five Standards

Not sequential steps. An integrated system.

Each standard is distinct. Each is essential. And each is strengthened by its relationship to the others. The power of SiA lies not in any single standard, but in the rigour of maintaining all five simultaneously.

01

Conditions

This standard examines the real change landscape honestly — not through a SWOT analysis or a list of known risks, but through a structured reading of the forces actually driving change and their specific implications for the organisation.

It builds a shared, evidence-based understanding across the leadership team of what is happening, what is driving it, and what the meaningful signals of change reveal about the trajectory ahead. It distinguishes between noise and signal. Between conditions that are transient and those that are structural.

A Board working this standard would be able to answer:
Q1

Which forces are reshaping our environment — and how do they interact?

Q2

What is the realistic trajectory for our operating environment?

Q3

Which wild cards could reshape our operating environment?

Q4

What does the change landscape mean specifically for us?

02

Sustainable Value Generation

Conditions change the value an organisation must generate — and widen the gap between the value an organisation believes it delivers and the value its stakeholders actually expect. This standard forces a clear-eyed conversation about what the organisation actually exists to do, for whom, and whether that value proposition is still relevant given current and emerging conditions.

It surfaces where value is being created, renewed, and quietly eroded — regularly exposing the gap between the value an organisation believes it delivers and the value its stakeholders actually need. It challenges assumptions at the level of strategic purpose, not presentation.

A Board working this standard would be able to answer:
Q1

Is the value we generate still the value our stakeholders need?

Q2

Where is value eroding before it shows up in our metrics?

Q3

Which parts of our value proposition need to be renewed — or retired?

Q4

Where are we at risk of optimising toward irrelevance?

03

Strategic Opportunity & Risk

With conditions and value in sharp focus, this standard surfaces the conditions with the most significant implications for the organisation’s forward trajectory. Consequential, direction-setting choices must be made about which SORs to actively pursue and which to leave alone.

This is different from conventional risk management. SOR thinking integrates both the opportunities a changing environment opens and the risks it carries — including the often-invisible risk exposed when opportunities are not taken, or a do-nothing path is pursued by default. It asks which choices are consequential enough to warrant deliberate Board-level attention — and which are being managed at the wrong level of the organisation.

A Board working this standard would be able to answer:
Q1

Which conditions will most determine our future relevance — for better or worse?

Q2

Which strategic risks are we underweighting — including the risk of doing nothing?

Q3

Which choices are consequential enough to demand our Board’s attention?

Q4

Which interdependencies are we missing that conventional tools can’t surface?

04

Capability Investment

A clear view of the strategic opportunity-risk landscape creates a different basis for capability investment decisions. This standard provides a deliberate view of the capabilities to build, acquire, or retire — grounded in what conditions require, not what the organisation has historically invested in.

The most crucial move is to build capability that serves now — and what comes next.

It asks what the organisation needs to be able to do that it cannot do today — and which capabilities are being over-invested in because they were valuable in conditions that no longer exist. Most organisations discover they are simultaneously under-building the capabilities they need and over-investing in those they no longer do.

A Board working this standard would be able to answer:
Q1

What must we be able to do that we cannot do today?

Q2

Which capabilities are we over-investing in for conditions that no longer exist?

Q3

What do we build, buy, borrow, or retire?

Q4

Where are our capability gaps widening silently?

05

Catalytic Action

This standard translates strategic clarity into targeted action — selecting initiatives specifically for their multi-pronged impact. Each action simultaneously builds capability, tests strategic assumptions, creates new value, and generates the feedback the organisation needs to keep adapting.

It moves fast enough to generate real learning before conditions change again — keeping the organisation’s strategy alive not as a document reviewed annually, but as a system continuously informed by what is actually happening.

A Board working this standard would be able to answer:
Q1

Which actions will have multi-pronged strategic impact?

Q2

How fast can we put an action into the world and learn from it?

Q3

What is each action teaching us about the real conditions?

Q4

How do we keep strategy moving as fast as our environment is?

The Algorithm

Five standards. One integrated system.

The SiA Algorithm visualises how the five standards operate together. The power is not in any single standard but in the discipline of maintaining all five simultaneously — and in understanding how each standard informs and strengthens the others.

This is what makes SiA fundamentally different from frameworks that treat strategy as a linear process. In conditions of structural instability, linear processes create the illusion of control while conditions continue to shift beneath them.

SiA acknowledges the complexity and builds a system that is designed to operate within it — not to pretend it away.

The algorithm was first published in Disrupted (2015), co-authored by Larry Quick and David Platt. It has been refined through 10+ years of application with Boards and Executives across Australian organisations navigating genuine complexity.

The Outcome

Making your organisation the strategist.

Not dependency. Capability.

The goal of SiA is not that your organisation requires Resilient Futures to navigate uncertainty. The goal is that your leadership team develops the thinking, tools, and behaviours to remain strategically effective as conditions keep shifting — without us in the room.

This is why capability building is embedded into every SiA engagement from the outset. We are not building client dependency; we are building client capacity.

Clarity, not complexity

SiA makes the complex navigable. Not simple — the complexity is real. But navigable, because the organisation has a shared language and a structured system for understanding what is happening and why.

Speed, not paralysis

Organisations with SiA embedded make consequential decisions faster — because they have already done the work of understanding conditions, clarifying value, and identifying what is actually at stake.

Alignment, not fragmentation

When the Board, CEO, and Executive team share a common understanding of conditions, value, and strategic priorities, governance functions as intended: enabling, not impeding, the organisation’s ability to act.

Adaptability, not rigidity

A living strategy system that is designed to update itself in response to new information — not a static plan that requires a formal review cycle to acknowledge that conditions have changed.

Relevance, not decline

The antidote to Managed Adaptive Decline. Organisations operating on SiA do not drift toward irrelevance through incremental adaptation. They continuously retest their relevance against changing conditions.

Capability, not dependency

Every SiA engagement builds internal capability. The thinking, tools, and behaviours are transferred to the leadership team so that strategic rigour becomes an enduring internal discipline, not an external service.

“Our role is to work alongside your leadership team — bringing the thinking, challenging the assumptions, and transferring the tools — so your organisation builds real strategic capability over time. Not dependency.”

— David Platt · Co-founder, Resilient Futures
