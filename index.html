The Problem

Why traditional strategic planning is failing in disruptive conditions

The operating environment has changed. Disruptions are no longer episodic. Cause and effect are no longer traceable. Planning assumptions no longer hold long enough for execution to stabilise.

01

Strategy cycles too slow — plans are obsolete before execution begins

02

Weak signals and interdependencies missed until the response window has closed

03

Boards approve plans they cannot effectively govern, built on dissolved assumptions

04

Decision quality and leadership effectiveness erode under sustained disruptive pressure

05

Operational capability gaps in people, systems and infrastructure quietly compound

That world is gone. Not temporarily disrupted — structurally gone. Technological, geopolitical, regulatory, and social forces are now continuous, interconnected, and self-reinforcing. There is no stable baseline to return to. Cause and effect are no longer traceable in the linear way strategy models assume. The conditions that made annual planning cycles, scenario matrices, and SWOT analysis coherent tools no longer exist.

Using those tools in this environment does not produce imprecise strategy. It produces confidently wrong strategy. The rigour of the methodology gives leaders a false sense of alignment with reality. Most organisations are not failing to execute their strategy. They are expertly executing a strategy that is no longer fit for the conditions.

“

The most dangerous place any organisation can be is confidently executing a strategy that is no longer aligned to reality.

David Platt — Resilient Futures
