The Problem

Why traditional strategic planning is failing in disruptive conditions

The operating environment has changed. Disruptions are no longer episodic. Cause and effect are no longer traceable. Planning assumptions no longer hold long enough for execution to stabilise.

01

Strategy cycles too slow, plans are obsolete before execution begins

02

Weak signals and interdependencies missed until the response window has closed

03

Boards approve plans they cannot effectively govern, built on dissolved assumptions

04

Decision quality and leadership effectiveness erode under sustained disruptive pressure

05

Operational capability gaps in people, systems and infrastructure quietly compound

The stable baseline is gone. Not temporarily disrupted, but structurally gone. Technological, geopolitical, regulatory, and social forces are now continuous, interconnected, and self-reinforcing. There is no stable baseline to return to. Cause and effect are no longer traceable in the linear way strategy models assume. The conditions that made annual planning cycles, scenario matrices, and SWOT analysis coherent tools no longer exist.

Using those tools in this environment does not produce imprecise strategy. It produces confidently wrong strategy. The rigour of the methodology gives leaders a false sense of alignment with reality. Most organisations are not failing to execute their strategy. They are expertly executing a strategy that is no longer fit for the conditions.

The most dangerous place any organisation can be is confidently executing a strategy that is no longer aligned to reality.

David Platt · Resilient Futures

Methodology

Strategy in Action: a decision-making framework for complex, unpredictable conditions

Making your organisation the strategist.

Grounded in complex adaptive systems thinking, resilience science, and cognitive science, Strategy in Action (SiA) operates through five interconnected standards. Not sequential or linear steps, but an integrated system that keeps strategy aligned to changing conditions at every level of the organisation.

01
Conditions

Building a shared, evidence-based and foresight-informed understanding of the real change landscape and what it means for your organisation.

02
Sustainable Value Generation

Clarifying the value the organisation must generate, protect, and renew, given the conditions it now faces and those it must prepare for.

03
Strategic Opportunity-Risk

Identifying the opportunities and risks that matter most, and making the deliberate choices that the change landscape requires.

04
Capability Investment

Building the capabilities needed now, while investing in those that will be required next.

05

Targeted initiatives with multi-pronged impact that generate fast feedback and keep strategy moving at the pace of change.
